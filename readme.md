# Time-Frequency Analysis: Polynomial Chirplet Transformation and Its Application in Instantaneous Frequency Estimation [with MATLAB Code]

The concept of instantaneous frequency (IF) was introduced by Carson and Fry in 1937 for analyzing single-component modulated signals, which was further developed by Delp and Gabor. Currently, Ville [4] has proposed a widely accepted definition of IF, unifying the work of Carson and Fry [1] and Gabor [3]. According to Ville's definition, the IF of a signal can be calculated from its derivative of the phase of its analytical signal.
The concept of IF plays an important role in various signal studies, ranging from radar [5], sonar [6], biomedical engineering [7], seismic surveying [8], speech and music [9] to automotive signals [10]. IF is commonly used to characterize significant physical parameters of signals. A variety of IF estimation methods have been developed; Boashash [11], [12] provide comprehensive overviews of these, including the interpretation of the IF concept itself and literature on IF estimation methods published before 1992. Generally speaking, mid-frequency estimation methods can be categorized into five types: 1) Mid-frequency estimators based on phase differences; 2) Mid-frequency estimators crossing zeroes; 3) Adaptive mid-frequency estimators based on linear predictive filters; 4) Mid-frequency estimators based on time-frequency distribution moments; and 5) Mid-frequency estimators based on time-frequency distribution peaks. Among these five categories of IF estimators, using TFD can yield more reliable and robust results against noise. Therefore, methods based on the finite difference time domain have received widespread attention and have seen rapid development.
The capability of the TFD method depends on the characteristics of the signal energy concentration at IF and its surroundings in the time-frequency plane. Three methods, namely Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) [13], Continuous Wavelet Transform (CWT) [14] and [15], and Wigner-Ville distribution (WVD) [16] are commonly used to generate the TFD for signals. STFT and CWT are essentially linear transformations that feature a static resolution in the time-frequency plane, which is divided into basic units with constant area. However, due to the limitations of the Heisenberg-Gabor inequality, neither STFT nor CWT can achieve good resolution in both the time domain and frequency domain, and good time resolution inevitably implies poor frequency resolution. Therefore, the time-frequency representations obtained by these two methods are only meaningful under certain time-frequency resolution. Thus, STFT and CWT can never produce the true time-frequency pattern of a signal, and thus can only provide limited measurement accuracy estimates for the IF of the signal, especially when the IF trajectory is a nonlinear function of time. To improve estimation accuracy, reassignment techniques have been proposed [17]. WVD is a quadratic transformation that can achieve high-precision estimation for noisy signals. When the signal has a linear frequency pattern and constant amplitude, its WVD will reduce to a row of delta functions along a linear IF trajectory. However, in the case of noisy signals or nonlinear IF patterns, the WVD based on peak estimation will be biased, and it has been shown that the trade-off between bias and variance is inevitable. The bias caused by the nonlinearity of IF is proportional to the power of the window length, while the variance caused by noise is a decreasing function of the window length. Efforts have been made to enhance the ability of WVD to estimate nonlinear IF better, mainly through adjusting the window length. Stankovic and Katkovnik [18] proposed an adaptive window length WVD, and the same author also proposed a WVD using variable and data-driven window lengths [19]. Algorithms based on TFD peak values have also been applied to other WVD representations, including polynomial WVD [20] and [21], which are proven to be unbiased for nonlinear IF trajectories, as well as pseudo and smooth pseudo-WVD [22] aimed at suppressing cross terms caused by noise in the time-frequency plane.
In addition to the TFD analysis method mentioned earlier, Linear Frequency Modulation (LFM) wavelet transform (CT) [23] is another TFD method specifically designed for analyzing linearly frequency modulated signals with linear IF patterns. When the IF trajectory of the considered signal is a nonlinear function of time, CT cannot guarantee an improvement in analysis accuracy. Therefore, Chassande-Mottin and Pei [24] suggest using LFM wave chain to process gravitational wave signals that are essentially quasi-periodic FM signals. Angrisani and D'Arco [25] proposed a modified version of CT by introducing additional curvature parameters into the traditional CT, which has proven particularly effective for signals with higher dynamic IF patterns. This paper introduces a new CT called Polynomial CT (PCT). Based on PCT, a program was developed to estimate the IF of signals with highly nonlinear IF trajectories. The PCT is developed by replacing the LFM kernel in CT with a new polynomial nonlinear IF kernel. In mathematics, the Weierstrass approximation theorem [26] ensures that any continuous function on a bounded interval can be uniformly approximated by polynomials at any desired precision. Therefore, PCT can perform high-precision analysis of various signals where the IF trajectory is any continuous function of time.

The process of IF estimation based on PCT is established in Section 3. The effectiveness of the newly proposed mid-frequency estimator is validated in Section 4 by applying it to several signals. The conclusions are presented in Section 5.
